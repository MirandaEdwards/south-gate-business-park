Blasting Information

Blasting, Vibration, and Monitoring

Plain-language information about why blasting is required and how monitoring is completed.

Why is blasting occurring?

Blasting is being carried out to support future road construction and site preparation activities. Material generated from blasting is being reused where practical for future road construction and site grading.

How is blasting monitored?

Independent vibration and air overpressure monitoring may be completed during blasting activities using specialized seismograph equipment. The purpose of monitoring is to record actual blast vibration and air overpressure levels at selected nearby locations.

August 4, 2026 monitoring summary

Independent monitoring was completed at two nearby residences. The reported peak particle velocity results were 3.175 mm/s at 2828 Joseph Street and 9.398 mm/s at 3259 Long Lake Road. The monitoring report notes that OPSS 120 recommends vibrations from blasting operations be kept below 50 mm/s at the observation point located at the closest residence to the blast site.

Air overpressure levels were reported as 120.1 dBL at 2828 Joseph Street and 115.7 dBL at 3259 Long Lake Road.

What should residents know?

Residents who have questions about blasting, vibration, dust, or site work are encouraged to contact the project team. Concerns will be reviewed and documented as part of the project communication and response process.
